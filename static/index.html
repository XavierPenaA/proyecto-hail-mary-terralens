1 Top Command Bar Controls (What the Toggles Do)

Use the top bar to switch locations, variables, and apply two critical scientific data-hygiene filters:

  • Remove ML Extremes (Acker Outlier Test): When turned ON, TerraLens identifies the top 5% most extreme anomalous months (such as the 1997–98 El Niño flood or the late-2023 drought peak) flagged by our 3-model ML consensus and removes them before recalculating the 45-year trend. Why? Because a single massive flood or drought near the start or end of a satellite record can create a fake long-term trend. If the trend stays statistically significant even after removing those extremes, the long-term shift is structural.
  • CERES 2001+ Guard (Satellite Instrument Boundary): NASA's surface solar radiation record (`ALLSKY_SFC_SW_DWN`) switches instruments in 2001 from older ERBE/SRB radiometers (1984–2000) to modern CERES radiometers (2001–present). Joining both eras blindly creates an artificial step-change around 2000. Turning CERES 2001+ Guard ON restricts solar radiation trend tests to the homogeneous 2001–2025 CERES era so instrument splicing is never mistaken for climate change.
  • Region, Parameter & Year Window: Choose any of the 11 benchmark regions across the Americas, select from 6 hydroclimatic variables, or adjust the start/end years (`1981–2025`) to test sub-period sensitivity.
2 What Each Workspace Tab Shows

The sidebar organizes the investigation into six complementary views:

  • Long-Term Trends (`Trends`): Displays deseasonalized monthly Z-score anomalies, OLS vs. Theil-Sen slopes, Pettitt regime breakpoints, the 12-month seasonal cycle, and the multi-variable False Discovery Rate (BH-FDR) table.
  • Extreme Events & Drivers (`ML`): Shows the 2D multivariate phase space (`PCA 1 × PCA 2`) separating compound droughts from floods, plus feature attribution ranking which lagged physical drivers (`0 to 6 months`) control soil moisture.
  • Model Performance (`CV`): Compares 3 gradient-boosted & tree ensembles trained across 55,697 observations using strict chronological 5-fold `TimeSeriesSplit` cross-validation.
  • Outlier Sensitivity (`Acker`): Side-by-side comparison of baseline 45-year slopes vs. ML-filtered slopes across all 6 variables at once.
  • Regional Comparison (`Dipole`): Overlays two regions alongside the NOAA Oceanic Niño Index (ONI) to reveal opposite ENSO teleconnections (e.g., Coastal Ecuador floods vs. Amazon droughts).
  • Regional Map & NDVI (`50km–250m`): Combines the 96-cell NASA POWER regional trend map (`~50 km`), 1 km Meteomatics topographic series, 250 m Terra MODIS NDVI canopy stress, and a live global `(lat, lon)` coordinate probe.
3 How to Read the Statistical Metrics

Climate time series violate standard textbook assumptions. TerraLens reports both naive and corrected metrics side by side:

  • Theil-Sen Slope vs. OLS Slope: Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) is easily pulled by extreme El Niño spikes. The Theil-Sen estimator takes the median of all pairwise slopes across the 45-year record, making it resistant to isolated outliers.
  • Lag-1 Persistence (`r₁`) & Effective Sample Size (`n_eff`): A warm or dry month tends to be followed by another warm or dry month (`r₁ > 0`). Following Santer et al. (2000), 540 monthly observations with `r₁ = 0.35` only carry about `259` independent degrees of freedom (`n_eff`).
  • Hamed-Rao `p` & Benjamini-Hochberg `q`: `Hamed-Rao p` inflates variance to account for autocorrelation so false positives are eliminated. `BH-FDR q` controls the family-wise false discovery rate when testing all 6 variables simultaneously.
  • Pettitt Change-Point Date: Detects the exact month where the time series shifts from one mean regime to another (`step Δ`).

Unir ambas eras sin filtro crea un salto artificial alrededor del año 2000. Al mantener Filtro CERES 2001+ activo, las pruebas de radiación solar usan la era homogénea 2001–2025 para no confundir un cambio de satélite con cambio climático.

3 Cómo interpretar las métricas estadísticas

Las series climáticas tienen memoria temporal y valores extremos. TerraLens reporta las métricas clásicas y las corregidas:

  • Pendiente Theil-Sen vs. Pendiente OLS: La regresión lineal clásica (OLS) se distorsiona fácilmente con un superevento de El Niño. El estimador no paramétrico Theil-Sen calcula la mediana de todas las pendientes entre pares de puntos a lo largo de 45 años, siendo inmune a picos aislados.
  • Persistencia Lag-1 (r₁) y Tamaño Efectivo de Muestra (n_eff): Un mes cálido o seco suele ir seguido de otro similar (r₁ > 0). Según Santer et al. (2000), 540 meses con r₁ = 0.63 equivalen a solo 123.2 grados de libertad independientes (n_eff).
  • Valor p de Hamed-Rao y q de Benjamini-Hochberg: Hamed-Rao p corrige la varianza por autocorrelación para evitar falsos positivos. BH-FDR q controla la tasa de falsos descubrimientos al evaluar las 6 variables a la vez.
  • Cambio de Régimen de Pettitt: Detecta el mes exacto donde la serie salta de un promedio histórico a un nuevo régimen climático (Δ).

1 The Problem It Attacks (Statistical Traps in Raw Climate Series)

Calculating a standard linear regression directly on raw grid series often produces convincing but misleading trend claims across three well-documented pitfalls:

  • Trap #1 — Autocorrelation Inflation: Monthly soil moisture remembers prior months (r₁ > 0.6). Treating 540 correlated months as independent samples produces false p < 0.05 alerts where Hamed-Rao / Santer adjustments prove zero secular trend (e.g., Paute soil moisture drops from naive p = 0.030 to p = 0.728).
  • Trap #2 — Satellite Instrument Splicing: NASA POWER surface solar radiation switches from ERBE/SRB (1984–2000) to CERES radiometers in Jan 2001. Unfiltered trends mistake that calibration jump (1999-05 break, p = 1.10e-16) for dimming or brightening.
  • Trap #3 — Outlier & Scale Aliasing: Extreme El Niño spikes pull OLS slopes, while ~55 km pixels average out dry Andean valleys next to wet eastern slopes—requiring Acker outlier filtering and 250 m / 1 km multi-scale fusion.
2 How We Solved It (Methods & Space Data Used)

TerraLens attacks the 45-year climate detective challenge by pairing open space agency telemetry with strict bias-removal algorithms:

  • 45-Year Multi-Sensor Fusion: Integrates NASA POWER (1981–2025 across 11 global regions + a 96-cell Ecuador grid), Terra MODIS 250 m NDVI canopy passes, NOAA ONI teleconnections, and 1 km Meteomatics terrain series.
  • Anti-Bias Trend Auditing: Removes seasonal cycles via monthly Z-scores, computes outlier-resistant Theil-Sen slopes, applies Hamed-Rao & Santer persistence corrections (n_eff), locates Pettitt regime shifts, and runs Benjamini-Hochberg FDR across all variables.
  • Ensemble ML & Outlier Sensitivity: Combines three unsupervised detectors (IsolationForest, LOF, EllipticEnvelope) to flag compound extremes, then re-tests every 45-year slope without extremes (Acker Test) plus a zero-leakage 1981–2018 vs. 2019–2025 future benchmark.
  • Live Global Probe: Allows scientists and decision-makers to click any coordinate on Earth to run the full NASA POWER retrieval and trend audit on demand.

1 ¿Qué problema ataca TerraLens? (Trampas Estadísticas en Series Brutas)

Aplicar una regresión lineal común directamente sobre series espaciales crudas suele inducir conclusiones erróneas por tres trampas metodológicas concretas:

  • Trampa #1 — Inflación por Autocorrelación: La humedad del suelo recuerda meses anteriores (r₁ > 0.6). Tratar 540 meses como independientes produce falsos positivos (p < 0.05) que desaparecen al corregir la varianza con Hamed-Rao y Santer (ej. Paute pasa de p = 0.030 a p = 0.728).
  • Trampa #2 — Quiebres Instrumentales de Satélite: La radiación solar en NASA POWER cambia de radiómetros ERBE/SRB (1984–2000) a sensores CERES en enero de 2001. Sin filtrar esa frontera, un salto de calibración (1999-05, p = 1.10e-16) parece cambio climático.
  • Trampa #3 — Sesgo por Extremos y Escala Gruesa: Un pico extremo de El Niño arrastra la pendiente clásica OLS, y un píxel de ~55 km promedia valles secos andinos con laderas amazónicas húmedas—exigiendo el Test de Acker y fusión 250 m / 1 km.
2 ¿Cómo se resolvió? (Herramientas, Datos NASA y Método)

TerraLens ataca el reto conectando telemetría espacial abierta con cuatro capas de auditoría para separar señal física de ilusiones estadísticas:

  • Fusión Multi-Sensor de 45 Años: Integra series reales NASA POWER (1981–2025 en 11 nodos globales + malla Ecuador de 96 celdas), vegetación Terra MODIS a 250 m (NDVI), teleconexión NOAA ONI y series topográficas a 1 km (Meteomatics).
  • Auditoría Estadística Anti-Sesgo: Desestacionaliza cada mes en Z-scores, calcula pendientes robustas Theil-Sen, corrige la varianza por autocorrelación (Hamed-Rao y Santer n_eff), detecta saltos estructurales (Pettitt) y controla falsos descubrimientos (BH-FDR).
  • Machine Learning y Test de Acker: Un consenso de 3 detectores (IsolationForest, LOF, EllipticEnvelope) aísla los eventos compuestos extremos y recalcula las tendencias sin ellos, respaldado por un split temporal sin fugas (1981–2018 entrenamiento vs. 2019–2025 futuro oculto).
  • Sonda Global en Vivo: Permite hacer clic en cualquier coordenada de la Tierra para auditar al instante la serie NASA POWER y distinguir cambio climático real de ruido o saltos de sensor.
